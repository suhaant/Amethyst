## Slide 2 · The problem (`Slide2Problem`)

- Eyebrow: THE PROBLEM
- Title: High-risk wounds get infected, and nobody notices in time.
- Hero stat (count up): **60%** of diabetic foot ulcers become infected.
- Three supporting facts, small, in a row:
  - 15–20% of diabetic foot infections end in amputation
  - $28–32B a year in Medicare wound costs (8.2M beneficiaries)
  - Nerve damage removes pain, the body's warning sign
- Contrast line (muted): Everyday cuts get infected only 2.6–3.5% of the time. The danger is high-risk wounds.
- Source: Diabetes Care; Nussbaum et al., Value in Health 2018; emergency department laceration studies

## Slide 3 · How it works (`Slide3Solution`)

- Eyebrow: OUR SOLUTION
- Title: A patch that senses, predicts, and treats.
- Five steps, revealed left to right with a thin connecting line:
  1. **Sense:** pH, moisture and temperature every few minutes
  2. **Predict:** an ML model scores risk as Normal, Warning or Infection
  3. **Break biofilm:** pulsed `40 kHz` ultrasound
  4. **Kill bacteria:** `405 nm` violet light, including MRSA, no drugs
  5. **Alert:** the phone app notifies the patient and care team
- Side detail: healthy wounds sit at pH 4–6; infected wounds turn alkaline (7.5–9).
- Footnote: Built-in safety: pulsed therapy, a skin-temperature cutoff, and daily dose limits.
